# Act II script: the incorrect rule (draft 3, for the author)

*Date: 2026-10-05. Follows docs/specs/2026-10-05-act-2-wrong-guidance.md (the six beats) and the design
spec §2 and §4. Same page and line rhythm as Act I: at most three lines a page, at most 26 words a
line; word counts in brackets. Under each line, in italics, what is on screen and what the camera does.
Drafts 2 and 3 apply the author's notes of 2026-10-05 and 2026-10-06 (see "Changes" at the end).*

## What Act II is for

Act I spent one close look on one drawing. Act II shows where a close look pays most across many pieces
of work: the few things at the top that every agent reads, because a rule that is wrong but agrees with
everything built from it gets copied faster than a person can find the copies, and the only fix is at
the source.

**From Act I:** the correct bike is riding when Act II opens; its particles reflow into the first stack
of cubes as the camera pulls away, and the abstract world grows out of that. **To the Coda:** the last
line, "the higher up a thing sits, the longer you look at it", is the Coda's map said in one sentence.

## Words

The prose names things by what they are, not by how the picture draws them (author, 2026-10-05): rules,
plans, code, a defect, a reviewer. The picture keeps its own vocabulary in the stage directions only:
stacks of cubes, streams, and the hairline crack that shows a defect up close.

## Labels on the layers

**No labels** (the author, 2026-10-06). Pages 2 to 4 name the three layers in the lines, one scene each,
in the order the camera visits them; after that the shapes carry it, and later lines say "at the top",
"down here".

## The script

### Page 1

Now let's zoom out. (4)

*The camera begins to pull away from the riding bike. Its particles loosen and reflow into a small
stack of cubes: the bike becomes the first stack.*

You want a hundred things built, and the agents build all day. (12)

*More stacks come up around it in the dark, near and far, and the camera keeps pulling back.*

### Page 2

Each piece of work is built from what its agent reads. (11)

*Medium shot of a few stacks. Fine streams run into them, and a cube settles each time a stream arrives.*

At the top, the rules every agent reads before it starts. (11)

*The camera rises to the top layer: a handful of large stacks, slow to change, with many streams leaving
them.*

### Page 3

In the middle, the plans: the instructions for one piece of work. (12)

*The camera drops to the middle layer: more stacks, smaller, each fed by a stream from above.*

### Page 4

At the bottom, the code, written from the plans. (9)

*The camera drops again to the bottom layer: many small stacks, quick to appear.*

### Page 5

Rules shape plans, and plans shape code. (7)

*The camera pulls far back until all three layers are in view, streams running downward the whole way.*

### Page 6

From far away, everything looks right. (6)

*Very wide, slow orbit. The whole network, three layers deep, growing. Every stack has the same glow.
Nothing is marked.*

### Page 7

Come closer. Down here, one piece of code has a defect. (11)

*The camera dives to one small stack in the bottom layer. In one cube, a bright jagged line that was
invisible a moment ago.*

Up close, you can see it. So you fix it. (10)

*Hold close. The person's work lands on the cube and rebuilds it. The line closes; the stack is whole.*

### Page 8

Now step back. (3)

*The camera pulls out to the wide shot. The network has grown while we were close.*

The agents kept building. Whatever is built on a defect carries it too. (13)

*A highlight picks out the defective stacks, spreading down and sideways from the plan the fixed one
came from. Some are copies of a neighbour.*

Twelve new defects. You fixed one. (6)

*Exactly twelve highlighted stacks. The fixed one sits dark among them.*

### Page 9

One agent wrote down what it built, so the next agent would know. Now the defect is in the rules too. (21)

*A thin stream climbs from a defective stack, up through the plans, to the top layer, where a new big
stack forms with the crack in it.*

### Page 10

You cannot fix them one at a time as fast as they are made. (14)

*Wordless first: a quick dive to a second defective stack, it is rebuilt, a quick pull-out. Then hold
wide as the line comes up: the highlighted tree has grown, down, sideways and up, and new defective
stacks keep appearing; the two fixed ones are lost among them.*

### Page 11

So you ask a reviewer to look at every new piece of work before it is kept. (17)

*A small ring of light passes over each new stack as it forms, pauses, and moves on.*

The reviewer says they are all fine. (7)

*The ring passes over a defective stack and leaves it glowing like the rest. And the next. And the next.*

The reviewer read the same rule. (6)

*A stream from above feeds the ring. The camera starts to follow it up.*

### Page 12

Follow the defects back up. (5)

*The camera climbs the highlighted lineage through the layers. Branches merge as it rises.*

They all lead to one place at the top: one incorrect rule. (12)

*The source: a large top-layer stack. Up close, one cube with a bright jagged line. Every highlighted
branch converges on it. Then, still under this line, the camera pulls back a little and the source's
streams fan out, down into the plans, across to the reviewer's ring, and over to the second defective
stack at the top: one mistake, copied, with no words needed.*

### Page 13

Fix the rule. (3)

*The person's work rebuilds the cracked cube in the source. The line closes.*

And fix the rule an agent wrote from it, or the defect will spread again. (15)

*The camera pans to the second defective stack at the top; it is rebuilt too.*

Now new work built from them is correct. (8)

*New streams leave both; new stacks form below with no crack in them.*

### Page 14

Then trace everything built from the incorrect rule, and fix what it already made. (14)

*The highlight runs down the lineage from the source, and each defective stack is rebuilt as it is
reached, top layer to bottom. The highlight goes out.*

### Page 15

The higher up a thing sits, the longer you look at it. (12)

*Wide from just above the top layer: the few big stacks, and the whole network fanning out beneath
them. The camera settles and holds. The Coda's map comes up over it.*

## Choices I made

- **II.1 (zoom out):** kept only the pull-away on page 1, with the bike's particles reflowing into the
  first stack. No workshop of bike copies.
- **II.2 (the layers):** collapsed from seven tiers to three, one scene each (pages 2–4), then a wide
  shot of how they connect (page 5). Pace layering is seen (big and slow at the top, small and quick at
  the bottom), not said.
- **II.4 (overload) cut:** a different failure that would compete with the defect. **II.5 (promotion)**
  survives only as the up-flow on page 9, told as the risk. **II.6 (meta)** is cut; its one sentence is
  the last line.
- **Colour:** no colour or dot words anywhere. Who did what is carried by the verbs ("you fix it", "the
  agents build") and by distance (the camera is close when it is you).
- **The tally** ("twelve new defects") is in page 8's last line; the storyboard must show exactly twelve.
- **No fence** at the source's outflow, in the text or the picture (the author, 2026-10-06).
