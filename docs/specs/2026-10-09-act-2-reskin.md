## 1. Why

The author's verdict on the built Act II has two parts:

- The kinds of body look too alike: the rules at the centre, the plans and the code are all
  lattice spheres with a glowing rim.
- The galaxy's spiral looks weird. The tuner's critique gives the reasons: hard-edged arms of constant
  width, no disc between the arms, a flat radial law, no bulge, uniform blue, no knots, no dust lanes.

## 2. The author's model (binding)

- **The centre is a black hole made of all the rules together.** It is one continuous mass, with no
  discrete rule bodies, and every token is a piece of its matter.
- **Prompts are the matter falling in.** This is shown, never named.
- **The one incorrect rule** is a thread of bad matter, not a body: a single ember strand spiralling
  through the disk among clean blue.
- **up-1, "Now the defect is in the rules too":** one agent's notes fall in as an ember stream and
  become that strand.
- **The broadcast:** ember knots fire up the jets. This is the bad rule going out to every agent.
- **The trace, "They all lead to one place at the centre: one incorrect rule.":** the lineage
  converges on the centre, and the strand is singled out.
- **The fix:** the strand cools to clean blue, and the ember knots stop.
- **A plan** is its star and its drawing (the author, 2026-10-10: R for one system, H for the
  neighbourhood; §4.7). The drawing is a schematic round the star, never in its place. The star is
  self-lit, with diffraction spikes:
  - a dense core and a falloff halo;
  - 4 or 6 spikes drawn as rows of dots, longer when the star is brighter;
  - colours from white to amber, never green or purple;
  - they pulse but do not travel.
- **Planets (code)** keep the lattice sphere, with a **terminator**:
  - lit on the side facing their own star and dark on the far side, a crescent made by dimming dots;
  - cool colours, tinted by their star;
  - moons follow the same rule.
- **Glow only where it is hot.** The hot things are the hole, the stars and anything lit ember.
  Planets and moons lose their glow, which is the icosphere's rim term. A lit planet keeps its ember point.
- **Moon size stays as it is.**

### 4.2 The black hole

**Frame and scale.** The hole sits at the galactic centre and turns with the galaxy. One unit of
distance is one horizon radius (`scale` 1):

- horizon 1;
- shadow (`B_CRIT`) 2.6;
- disk 2.75–12 (fading out over 10–12.5);
- jets from ±2.4 along the galactic ±y.

The old core lay within 12 units of the centre (`CORE_SPREAD`), so the disk fills the place the core
bodies filled. Tilt is 0: the disk lies in the galactic plane.

**Parts, with the inspector's values (all adopted):**

- **Lens** (`holeLens`, view space, about the hole's view-space centre `hv`):
  - Let `rel = (pv − hv)/scale`, `d = −rel.z` (the depth behind the hole), `b = |rel.xy|`,
    `deff = max(d, 0) + 0.45 e^{−|d|}` and `disc = √(b² + 8·bend·deff)`. **bend = 0.6.**
  - **Image 0:**
    - `b' = ½(b + disc)`;
    - captured (hidden) when `d > 0` and `b' < B_CRIT`;
    - magnified ×(1 + 0.45·smoothstep(0, 3, d)).
  - **Image 1** (the far side's underside arc, only where `d ≥ 0.4`):
    - `b' = ½(disc − b)`, pulled to `B_CRIT + (b' − B_CRIT)·0.35`;
    - mirrored (`t = −t`);
    - gain 0.55.
  - **The pile-up:** anything inside the shadow piles onto its rim. This is not a D-shape.
    - `x = b' − B_CRIT`;
    - `b' = B_CRIT + 0.03 + softplus₁₀(x)`;
    - ok × mix(0.22, 1, smoothstep(−0.3, 0.45, x)).
  - **The depth:** z is flattened toward the hole's plane by `w = d > 0 ? 1 : e^{d/3}`.
- **Disk** (Kepler shear and Doppler lopsidedness):
  - Matter turns at the Kepler rate `ω(r) = 3.1·r^{−1.5}` (ω₀ **3.1**: about 10 s a turn at the inner
    edge). A rigid orbit per dot would shear the streaks into even grain within about 10 minutes, so the
    disk moves by **streak lifetimes** (below).
  - **Streak lifetimes** (`HOLE_DISK.motion`; the author's pick, "Recommended", 2026-10-10). The
    research is in
