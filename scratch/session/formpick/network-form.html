Motes of work · Act II · choosing a form

What should the network look like?

Rough mock frames at phone size. They are not the essay's renderer, but they show each option's composition honestly. Swipe each row sideways.

"Each stack being a brick wall one brick deep looks really boring and cheap, and why walls. … middle and bottom blurry as heck, top half just looks like a bunch of misshapen walls strewn about."

Why it looks like that now

Walls. Each piece's cubes were laid as one row of bricks, so every piece is a wall one brick deep, narrowing to a stepped triangle. Each was also turned to a random angle, which made them look strewn about.

Blur. Every piece has a soft round glow so that it reads from far away. Hundreds of glows overlap into fog. Every option below removes the glows entirely; a defect lights its cubes in ember with a brighter edge instead.

The camera fight. The three layers are wide discs stacked up high, which is a landscape shape on a portrait phone. Any pull-back drags the upper layers through the text.

Chosen: the galaxy, made of planets and moons

Each piece of work is a planet: a crisp shell of motes with one to six moons on dotted tracks. Code planets have one track and plan planets two. Rule planets have three tracks and four to six moons, so each is a little solar system. From overhead, each planet with its rings has the same shape as the galaxy itself, which gives the zoom out its powers of ten: one planet, then its system, then the galaxy. The planets stay in place and only the moons move. The soft round glows are gone.

The defect is a derp. Once in a while, one moon stalls, runs backwards, or skips off its track and snaps back, or the planet shivers. From far away the difference is a few pixels. Up close, watching against the dotted track, it is unmistakable. Healthy moons never derp. Anything built on a defect derps the same way, and the fix makes it settle.

1 · The shape of one piece (earlier options)

Every option uses whole cubes on a grid, so no piece is ever a wall. Each frame shows a code piece, a plan, a rule and a row of code far behind.

2 · The shape of the whole system

Each option is shown at three moments: the end of the zoom-out, "Rules shape plans…", and the twelve lit. All use the crate.

S3 Terraces recommended

Three steps climbing away from you, like a stair or a hillside of rice terraces. Code sits on the lowest, nearest, widest step, plans on the step behind and above it, and rules on the top step at the back, few and large. Streams fall over the steps' edges, and new work is born at the front edge, coming toward you.

  • Rules are always the top of the picture and code the bottom, from overhead or side-on, so nothing is ever dragged through the text. The powers-of-ten zoom-out works.
  • Portrait by nature, and every word of the script stays. The twelve count at a glance with nothing hiding them.
  • The reviewer works the front row, where new work arrives, and the final sweep of fixes runs toward you.
  • A stair says "hierarchy" out loud, which is slightly literal. The far-away shot sways instead of circling all the way round, because from behind the stair the order flips.

S1 Column

The same three layers, made tall and narrow: a tower of three clouds, few and big at the top, a dense swarm at the foot. This is the cheapest change.

  • The layers read clearly side-on; a full orbit still works.
  • It is still a cone. From overhead the rules sit on top of the code, so the zoom-out flies through them.

S2 Chandelier

The network laid out as the tree it is: each plan hangs under its rule, and each piece of code hangs under its plan.

  • Lineage is the layout itself, and the twelve are one visible branch.
  • It is the mound you already rejected, drawn better, and it is wide again at the base.

S4 Galaxy

Laid flat and seen from above: rules at the hub, plans on an inner ring, code out along spiral arms.

  • The most literal powers-of-ten zoom: one piece, then an arm, then a galaxy.
  • There is no top, middle or bottom. About six lines would change ("At the top" becomes "At the centre", and "The higher up a thing sits" becomes "The closer to the centre…"). From straight above, cubes lose their sides.

3 · The defect, from its shape alone

There is no colour or glow before the defect is found. The crack runs diagonally as a jagged bolt, wraps over the top edge, and is wide at the mouth and a hairline at the foot. The loose piece leans out about 8°, so the outline steps. The camera moves in under "Come closer" at a three-quarter angle, and the rest of the stack fades to 30%. Because the treatment is in the cube's own frame, it works on any of the piece shapes above.

What I need from you

  1. System: terraces (recommended), column, chandelier or galaxy?
  2. Piece: crate (recommended), cube of cubes, cairn or pillar?
  3. Defect: does the proposed crack read as broken to you?
  4. If terraces: is a slow sway acceptable for "From far away, everything looks right", in place of a full circle? And should it end on about 180 pieces of code (closer to "a hundred things") rather than 300?

Whatever you pick, the soft round glows go, the cube bodies being built now carry over, and the zig-zag crack applies to a single cube in any of these.
