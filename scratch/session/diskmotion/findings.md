# The disk in motion — research, analysis and prototype

Question from the author: what does a realistic, "Interstellar-y" accretion disk look like *in motion*, and what should the dot-drawn disk borrow — keeping the current disk's "interesting distribution of thin and dense trails"?

Hard constraints every proposal below respects: every frame is a pure function of (scroll, ambient clock); ambient = wall seconds since load wrapped at 3,600, frozen in tests and under reduced motion; no simulation state, no integration, no `Math.random`; per-dot randomness from seeded hashes; positions computed in the vertex shader with a TypeScript twin checked by parity tests.

Sources are tiered: T1 direct test/computation here, T2 papers/official sources, T3 blogs/talks, T4 unverified. Reference images and stills are in `img/` (internal only, never committed).

**Short version.** The inspector's trails are a transient: Kepler shear turns them into structureless grain inside a few minutes (inner third of the disk in ~60 s, whole disk by ~10 min — §5). The fix that keeps them alive forever, is pure in t, costs a handful of hashes per dot and twins trivially is **streak lifetimes**: each streak lives `L_k = 3600/N_k` seconds, is born and dies under a brightness envelope, and is re-hashed each life from `(k, n mod N_k)`. The prototype (`proto.html`) has it as a toggle; the stills show the current model's trails gone at 2 min and the recommended model's trails identical in character at 0, 2, 10 and 60 min (§8).

## 0. The reference disk as built (T1: read of `bhmock/blackhole.html`)

- 26,000 disk dots. 360 streaks: radius `r = 2.75 + 9.25·u^1.6` (inward-biased: median r ≈ 5.8, a quarter of the streaks inside r ≈ 4.0), angle `a` uniform, length `len = (0.25 + 1.3u)(1 + r/12)` radians, brightness `b = 0.35 + 0.65u`, pitch `0.15 + 0.35u`.
- 66 % of dots belong to a streak: offset `off ∈ ±len/2` along it, `r_dot = max(2.75, s.r + N(0,1)·0.10 − off·pitch)`, `φ = s.a + off`, brightness `s.b·(0.5 + 0.7u)`. 34 % are a diffuse haze `r = 2.75 + 9.75·u^1.5`.
- Every dot moves rigidly on its own circle: `φ(t) = aPhi + 3.1·r^−1.5·t` (period 10.5 s at r = 3, 30 s at r = 6, 64 s at r = 10). Doppler beaming `g^(3·lop)`, colour from `g`, weak-field lens with a far-side underside image (image 1, at 0.55 brightness), softplus pile-up at the shadow rim.
- There is **no per-streak coherence in the shader**: a streak is only a correlation in the initial conditions. Nothing holds it together once dots at different radii drift apart. That is the whole degradation problem (§5).
- Phone scale at the inspector's framing (distance 75, fov 45, 844 CSS px tall): 13.6 CSS px per unit, 27 device px per unit at DPR 2. A streak of ~48 dots at r = 6 is 220 device px long, so even fresh it is ~0.2 dots per device px: the trails read as *correlated grain with voids between*, not as continuous lines. Shear therefore never draws visible rings here (there are too few dots per pixel for that); it just erases the correlation.

## 1. Simulated disks over time

**What the GRMHD movies show (T2).**
- The EHT's model library is 3D ideal-GRMHD runs from several codes (BHAC, H-AMR, iharm, KORAL), evolved to ≥ 10⁴ GM/c³ with a snapshot every 5 GM/c³ — "about 43 hours for an M87-mass black hole" per snapshot, so a movie frame is a few per cent of an ISCO orbit (EHT M87 Paper V, arXiv:1906.11242; PATOKA, arXiv:2202.11721). Most of the 1.3 mm emission comes from within ≲ 10 r_g, where the flow is in statistical steady state. Porth et al. 2019 (ApJS 243, 26) is the code comparison on the MRI-driven torus; its finding is that the codes agree as resolution rises — the turbulence is a converged, physical thing, not numerical noise.
- **MRI turbulence in motion.** Shearing-box two-point correlations (Guan, Gammie, Simon & Johnson 2009, arXiv:0901.0273) show the magnetic field "composed of narrow filaments that are swept back by differential rotation into a trailing spiral" — the filaments are tilted ~10–20° from azimuthal (trailing), a few scale heights long, and the correlation time of the turbulence is of order Ω⁻¹: injected energy "dissipates on a timescale of Ω⁻¹" (Simon, Hawley & Beckwith 2009, arXiv:0909.2003), i.e. a structure lives roughly a sixth of an orbit before it is replaced; a later shearing-box study finds the correlation time inversely proportional to the shear rate (arXiv:1507.04711). So a GRMHD disk is a field of short, trailing, *constantly renewed* filaments; at any instant the pattern looks much like the previous instant's, but no individual filament survives a full orbit.
- **Larger, slower structures.** In MAD (strongly magnetised) flows, magnetic flux eruptions expel under-dense, over-magnetised "flux tubes" or bundles that orbit at 5–40 r_g, substantially sub-Keplerian, and resist being sheared apart for a few orbits (Dexter et al. 2020, "Flares in the Galactic Centre I", arXiv:2006.03658; Porth et al. 2021, arXiv:2006.03658 companion; Ripperda et al.). The eruption cycle recurs every ~1,000–2,000 GM/c³ ("5–10 hours in the galactic centre", arXiv:2310.16938), i.e. every ~15–30 ISCO orbits. These are the big bright knots and the m = 1 lopsidedness that drift round the ring in the simulated movies. MAD runs are sub-Keplerian and more variable than SANE (EHT Sgr A* library, arXiv:2411.12647). Spiral density waves show up as two-armed standing shocks in *tilted* disks (Chatterjee et al. 2020, MNRAS 499, 362: the tilt "produces a two-arm shock" that changes the image morphology; movies in the paper's YouTube playlist).
- **Contrast.** The library papers quantify variability by light-curve modulation and image-structure statistics rather than a per-pixel contrast; the usable numbers are that the EHT ring's azimuthal brightness ratio is ~2:1 (M87 2017/2018) and that Sgr A*'s 230 GHz light curve varies by tens of per cent within hours (§2). In the NASA 2019 visualisation (a thin-disk toy, not GRMHD) the bright knots are a few times the local mean and stretch into "light and dark lanes" (§3).

**What to take from this.** Real disks in motion are not a frozen texture rotating; they are a *renewed* texture: short trailing filaments living ~1/6–1 orbit, a few long-lived bright knots living a few orbits, and a slowly wandering m = 1 asymmetry. That is exactly the shape of a closed-form "streak lifetimes" model, and it is why the Gargantua write-up's cross-fade (§3) is the right instinct with the wrong granularity.

## 2. Observations

- **GRAVITY 2018 (A&A 618, L10, T2).** Three bright near-infrared flares of Sgr A* showed the centroid moving clockwise in loops "on scales of typically 150 μas over a few tens of minutes, corresponding to about 30 % the speed of light", with the polarisation angle rotating with "the same 45 (±15) min period as that of the centroid motions". The fit is a compact hot spot on a near-face-on circular orbit at 6–10 r_g (just outside the ISCO of a non-spinning hole). The 2023 follow-up (arXiv:2301.05925) adds a fourth flare, period ~1 h, radius ~9 r_g, "consistent with Keplerian orbital motion"; it notes the *absence* of strong Doppler-boosting peaks in the light curves, i.e. the orbits are close to face-on. A 2026 preprint argues for multiple simultaneous hot spots in 340 GHz variability (arXiv:2605.11698, T4 unverified).
- **What it looks like.** One bright knot, a few r_g across, going round once in ~45–60 min (about 1.5–2 ISCO periods), brightening modestly on the approaching side (Doppler) and by lensing on the far side (Broderick, press interview, T3). It does not survive many orbits.
- **Sgr A* at the EHT (Paper IV, ApJL 930, L15, T2).** GM/c³ ≈ 20 s, so the ISCO period is ~30 min; the source varies on timescales "as short as 3.4 hr" on long baselines and is uncorrelated beyond a few hours; variability reaches "~100 % on some baselines"; the broadband light curve varies on minutes.
- **M87 across epochs (T2).** Ring diameter 42 → 43.3 μas, unchanged; but "the position angle of the brightness asymmetry in 2018 is shifted by about 30° relative to 2017" (A&A 681, A79, 2024), and 2009–2017 data show the peak's position angle varying year to year "as expected for a stochastic accretion flow" (Wielgus et al. 2020). With M87's ISCO period ~ weeks, a year is ~20–40 orbits: the lopsidedness is Doppler (fixed side) plus a wandering turbulent component. No EHT movie of M87 has been published as of this writing (the 2026 goal was funding-dependent; a Jan 2026 preprint, arXiv:2601.00394, instead compares the *distribution* of asymmetry across epochs with GRMHD).

## 3. Visualisations — how they animated the disk and what they chose

- **NASA SVS 13326, 2019 (Schnittman, T2).** A thin, hot disk around a *non-rotating* hole, rendered mass-invariant. The texture is explicitly knot-based: "Bright knots constantly form and dissipate in the disk as magnetic fields wind and twist through the churning gas", and differential rotation "stretches and shears the bright knots, producing light and dark lanes in the disk". Doppler beaming is kept (the approaching side brighter and bluer; it vanishes face-on and flips when the camera passes under the disk). The photon ring is "composed of multiple rings, which grow progressively fainter and thinner". The published movie is a camera orbit; the disk's own motion is the knots and lanes being sheared. (An earlier release had the camera mirrored, so beaming showed on the wrong side — corrected; a reminder that the *side* of the lopsidedness is a physical statement.)
- **NASA SVS 14576, 2024 "plunge" (Schnittman, T2).** Non-rotating hole, 4.3 M☉×10⁶; the disk is described only as "a flat, swirling cloud of hot, glowing gas" used "as a visual reference during the fall"; what moves in that piece is the *camera* (62 % c at the horizon, two orbits in 42 s, 10 TB, 5 days on 0.3 % of Discover) and the lensed sky; nothing is stated about how the disk's texture evolves.
- **Interstellar / DNGR (James, von Tunzelmann, Franklin & Thorne 2015, CQG 32, 065001, T2).** The disk was "created for Interstellar by Double Negative artists rather than created by solving astrophysical equations": an infinitely thin planar disk whose colour and optical thickness come from "an artist's image", a Houdini voxel volume for the main shots, and procedural texture only in close-ups ("much slower"). It is "not currently accreting" and sits at a constant 4,500 K. The fully relativistic version was "exceedingly lopsided, with the hole's shadow barely discernible, [and] was obviously unacceptable", so Doppler and gravitational shifts were dropped from the film's images and the spin was lowered from a/M ≈ 1 to 0.6 so the lensing would not be "too confusing for a mass audience"; a veiling lens flare measured from the real IMAX lenses was convolved on. The paper says nothing about animating the disk's texture; in the film the visible motion is the camera's and the slow swirl of the painted disk (T4 from memory — the paper excerpt available here is silent on it). The look the author calls "Interstellar-y" is therefore: *symmetric*, soft, creamy, slow, flared — the opposite of the GRMHD movies on every axis except the lensed arch.
- **Gargantua WebGL (jonasjavier.dev, T3).** Fractal noise "in log-radius coordinates that rotate with the material", Keplerian: "the inner edge spins about 35 times faster than the outer one". The failure the author hit is our §5 exactly: "the differential rotation winds the noise up forever" and within minutes "the disk turned into concentric rings thinner than a pixel". The fix: "two copies of the disk, half a cycle apart, fade into each other every 20 seconds", so "the winding never passes a limit and the handover can't be seen". Doppler kept at a tamed ~2:1 (cream vs copper), with a control to switch it off. Phone performance comes from accumulating 8 jittered frames and stepping resolution 1.0→1.25→1.5 only while the phone keeps pace.

**Artistic choices worth borrowing:** keep the Doppler lopsidedness but tame it to ~2:1 (Gargantua, NASA) rather than removing it (Interstellar); make the *texture* the thing that moves (knots that form, stretch and dissipate), not a rotating painting; renew the texture rather than letting shear run (everyone who animated a Keplerian texture hit the winding wall); keep the far-side arch and the underside arc; the photon ring is static.

## 4. The lensed image in motion

- **Secondary image of a hot spot (T2).** Consecutive-order images of a source arrive "after a delay time of ≈ π times the BH shadow radius" (in light-travel units), with a weak dependence on the spot's orbital radius and a stronger one on inclination (arXiv:2403.08862); in Kerr the secondary image is "a compact crescent" hugging the photon ring on the *opposite* side of the hole from the primary, demagnified exponentially by the lensing Lyapunov exponent (Yfantis, Palumbo & Mościbrodzka 2025, arXiv:2504.16218). For Sgr A* the delay is ~5 min (about 0.17 of the ISCO period), for M87* ~6 days.
- **In the mock's units (T1).** The mock's β = √(0.5/r) with ω = 3.1·r^−1.5 fixes c = 3.1·√2 = 4.38 r_s/s; the delay π·2.6/4.38 = **1.9 s**, i.e. 0.18 of the ISCO period — the same ratio as the real thing. The prototype applies this delay to the underside arc (image 1) as one uniform (`uDelay`), at zero cost.
- **Photon-ring light echoes (T2).** The expected autocorrelation peaks at ~5 min in Sgr A*'s light curve are *not* observed, explained if the source's own correlation time exceeds the echo delay (arXiv:2406.04176). Nothing to animate there that is grounded in data.
- **What could read at phone size.** The far-side arch over the shadow is the far side's *primary* image and already moves with the disk — it reads. The underside arc is a 1–2 px band at phone scale; a delayed, demagnified copy of a hot spot sliding along it is a 2-px blip that only a bright hot spot (brightness ×2.6 in the prototype) makes noticeable, and only when it happens to be on the far side. Keep the delay (it is free and correct) but do not count on it being seen; nothing in the photon ring will read.

## 5. Shear analysis (T1: `shear.py`, closed form plus a Monte Carlo on the reference's own generator)

Kepler: ω = 3.1·r^−1.5, dω/dr = −1.5·3.1·r^−2.5. A streak's dots span Δr ≈ ±0.2 (2σ of the 0.10 Gaussian) plus pitch·len (mean 0.325 × 0.9(1 + r/12)), so Δr_total ≈ 0.4 + 0.3(1 + r/12).

**Reading it.** The inner third of the disk is at its floor by ~60 s, the mid disk by ~300 s, and the whole disk's azimuthal contrast equals that of uniformly random phases by **~600 s (10 min)**; the outer streaks (r > 7) are the last to go, at 20–30 min. The disk does *not* become visibly ringed — at ≤ 0.5 dots per device px there is nothing for shear to draw rings with — it becomes an even, structureless grain. The stills confirm it (`img/sheet-current_reco_xfade.png`, top row): the trails are plainly thinner at 2 min and gone at 10 min. The "interesting distribution of thin and dense trails" is a transient of the first minute or two of a page view.

**Side finding — the hour wrap.** Rigid-orbit dots do not return to their t = 0 place at t = 3,600 (ω·3600 is not a multiple of 2π: max φ error π over the haze, T1 `wrap.py`), so a page open across the wrap sees every haze dot jump once an hour. Commensurate radii fix it for the inner disk only (r_m = (3.1·3600/2πm)^{2/3}: spacing 0.13 device px at r = 3 but 5.3 px at r = 12, which would show as rings), so the honest fix is to give the haze dots the same lifetime treatment as the streaks (§7, option A′). The lifetime model's streak dots return exactly (max error 5e−13).

## 6. Why the per-pixel field prototype (`particles/disk-field.html`) looks flatter

The field hashes each (ring, angular cell) independently — existence, radial jitter, angle, brightness and size are all separate hashes per candidate dot — so its dot pattern is a Poisson point process with only a *radial* density law: no two cells know about each other, so there are no 48-dot clusters sharing a radius, no trailing pitch that lets a cluster cross three rings, no streak-level brightness that makes one arc bright and its neighbour dim, and no voids (the reference's bimodal mix of 34 % thin haze and 66 % dense streaks leaves real gaps; a Poisson field's local density only ever varies by 1/√n per cell). Rigid rotation within each 0.35-unit ring then removes even the transient shear structure, there is no height jitter (`aH`) to break the plane into a band, and the per-dot brightness is `0.3 + 0.7·hash` on top of the same smooth radial law everywhere — so what remains is the lens, the Doppler lopsidedness and a radial gradient, which is a *shaded annulus made of grain*, not a disk made of trails. Streaks are expressible in the field (a run of cells sharing one hash, as §3.2 of that report says) but were not built; without them the two prototypes differ exactly by the thing the author likes.

## 7. Closed-form fixes that keep the trails alive forever — evaluated

All are pure in (t): nothing is integrated, every frame is `f(static attributes, t)`.

| | Mechanism | Twin / parity story | Cost per dot per frame | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Streak lifetimes** (prototype default) | Streak k has a static base radius `rb_k` and a life `L_k = 3600/N_k` where `N_k = round(3600/L_target(rb_k))`, `L_target = lifeMul · len/( |dω/dr| · Δr ) / shear` (the time for shear to stretch it by its own length, ×lifeMul). Phase `off_k = hash(k)·L_k`; `n = floor((t+off)/L)`, `τ = t+off − nL`, life seed `(k, n mod N_k)`. Per life, re-hash radius (±8 % about rb), angle, length, brightness, pitch, hot flag. Dot: `r = max(2.75, rc + g·0.1 − u·len·pitch)`, `φ = a + u·len + ω(rc)·τ + shear·(ω(r) − ω(rc))·τ`, brightness × envelope `smoothstep(0,0.18,τ/L)·(1 − smoothstep(0.72,1,τ/L))`. Haze dots keep rigid orbits (or get the same treatment, A′). | The placement is one function `diskDot(i, t)` of 5 static per-dot numbers (k, u, g, bf, rb) and t; the TS twin is a line-for-line port (`twin.py` here is that port in Python and reproduces the shader's positions to 5e−13 across the hour wrap). ## 8. The prototype — `proto.html`

A copy of the inspector with the look untouched; only the disk's motion changes. `three.module.js`/`three.core.js` are beside it. Serve the directory (`python3 -m http.server`) and open `proto.html`.

- **Controls** (sheet → "Disk motion"): `Current` / `Recommended: streak lifetimes` / `Two-phase cross-fade`; an **ambient slider** 0–3600 s (dragging it freezes the clock), `Freeze ambient`, and jump buttons `t = 0`, `2 min`, **`Jump to t = 10 min`**, `60 min`. Under "Look": `Streak life ×` (lifeMul, default 1.0), `Shear fraction` (0.5), `Hot-spot fraction` (0.02), `Underside arc delay` (1.9 s), `Inward drift per life` (0).
- **Hooks**: `__bh.model(0|1|2)`, `__bh.ambient(t)`, `__bh.freeze(bool)`, plus the inspector's own.
- **Stills** (phone 390×844 @ DPR 2, SwiftShader, scene "At rest", default framing; `img/`): `current-t0000/0120/0600/3599.png`, `reco-…`, `xfade-…`; contact sheet of the disk crops `img/sheet-current_reco_xfade.png` (rows: current, recommended, cross-fade; columns: 0 s, 2 min, 10 min, 60 min). Parameter sweep at t = 10 min in `img/sweep/` (`lm0.6/1.0/1.6/2.5` = life ×; `sh0.25/1.0`; `hot06`; `drift06`) with sheets.
- **Measured (T1).** Lit pixels (> 40/255) in the disk crop: current 45.4k/46.3k/47.2k/46.3k at 0/120/600/3599 s (flat — shear moves dots, it does not remove them, which is why a pixel count cannot see the degradation and the contrast metric is needed); recommended 42.6k/43.9k/43.7k/42.1k (flat, ~6 % lower because the envelope dims streaks at birth and death); cross-fade 34.2k/41.4k/43.5k/33.9k (the breathing). Frame time under SwiftShader 40–60 ms for both models (no measurable difference; the disk is 2 × 26k points).
- **What the stills show.** Row 1: the current trails are visibly weaker at 2 min and gone at 10 min. Row 2: the recommended disk has the same family of thin and dense trails at every time; it reads a little more "arc-like" than the current model's t = 0 because at any instant a third of the streaks are old and stretched — which is also what NASA's lanes and the GRMHD filaments look like. Life ×0.6 is the closest to the current t = 0 character (shorter trails, more of them fresh); ×2.5 turns the disk into nested arcs. **Default 1.0** is the compromise.
- **Not done here:** essay-level integration (integer PCG hashes, the `orbitTurns` reduction, the real `lens` twin), a TS parity spec, and the haze's hour-wrap fix (A′).

## 9. Ranked recommendation

1. **Adopt now — streak lifetimes (A), with hot spots at 1–2 % (C) and the free 1.9 s underside-arc delay (D).** Pure in t, no state, ~+30 % vertex ALU on 18k dots, five static floats per dot, a 40-line placement function with a line-for-line TS twin, exact at the hour wrap. Keeps the trails' distribution forever (life ×1.0, shear 0.5) and is the honest cartoon of what the simulations show: filaments that form, trail and dissolve. Snap each life to `3600/N` so `n mod N` makes t = 3,600 ≡ t = 0.
2. **Adopt with it — give the 34 % haze a long per-dot life** (A′ on the haze) so nothing pops at the hour wrap; or restrict the haze to r < 8 with commensurate radii (G). Cheap either way.
3. **Try later — inward drift per life (E), a longer life for hot spots, and a slow m = 1 wander** (a brightness term `1 + 0.15·cos(φ − φ₁(t))` with φ₁ drifting over ~20 min, pure in t) to echo M87's year-to-year lopsidedness shift. All zero-cost; none proven to read at phone size.
4. **Try later — the two-disk cross-fade (B)** only as a fallback if a per-streak envelope ever looks too busy; its breathing (−33 % at each handover) is measurable and was visible in the stills.
5. **Skip — photon-ring echoes, multiple photon rings, and any secondary-image choreography**: the ring is 1–2 px at phone size, the arc is 1–2 px, the secondary image is a demagnified crescent; nothing there survives DPR 2 at 120 pt. Also skip true full-rate Kepler inside a streak (shear 1.0): inner streaks live ~5 s and flicker.
6. **Skip — the per-pixel field for the disk's body** unless streaks are built into it as cell runs (§6); without them it is a shaded annulus.
