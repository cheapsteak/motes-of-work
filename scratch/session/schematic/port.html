7. Round 2: plan, not monitoring

The author's verdict on round 1: B and C look better; A reads as "monitoring" rather than "plan"; B's sweep goes. The principle for this round: a plan is prescriptive, drawn before the thing exists, and shows intent (intended positions, dimensions, tolerances, construction). Sweeps, live counts, blinking warnings and scan readouts are monitoring language, and every one of them is gone from the new concepts. Kept from round 1: dots only, phosphor, the band and legibility rules, numbers only, the level-of-detail plan (nothing at the far level), the three states, the swipe. The new concepts come first in the swipe; A–D stay behind them for comparison.

What "off plan" means in every mock. A defective body sits 0.22 rad further round its ring, 0.9 units outside it, and 1.2× its planned size (DRIFT in mock.html). Every plan mark is drawn at the planned position and size; the body is drawn where it is. Healthy bodies coincide with their marks exactly, so nothing on a healthy system is an event. The one ember mark a concept may add is the departure itself (a second dimension, an ember band edge, a deviating arc, an ember stalk or tick), never a lamp, a counter or a glyph. The star still lights ember at df-5 as the essay has it; that is the body, not the schematic.

Level of detail, revised. Far: nothing. Low (40–110 px): the outer ring only, plus the concept's ember departure where one exists. Mid (110–300 px): every ring, thin, plus stalks or placeholders. Full (≥ 300 px, at most two systems): bands, graduations, dimensions, outlines and the planned values.

E · Placeholders

Description
Faint wireframe outlines of the planets and moons the plan intends, at their intended places and sizes: a screen-facing silhouette circle, an equator and a meridian facing the star. The code fills its outline. Rings stay as plain dotted circles.
The defect
The body does not fit: it sits beside its outline, drifted round and out, and overflows the size. The outline stays. Nothing turns ember except the body itself.
Beats
ly-3: the outlines draw in empty, the plan before the thing exists; ly-4: the bodies fill them; df-1/df-2: one body is seen beside its outline when you look; df-5/up-2: ember bodies beside empty outlines across the neighbourhood.
Risks
The quietest defect signal: at the hood the drift is 3 px, so the empty circle and the ember dot touch; the reader has to look. Outlines on a healthy body add a ring of dots round every planet, which can read as a glow the re-skin removed. 772 dots close, 2,294 at the hood.

F · Construction

Description
Orbits drawn as a drafter constructs them: a crosshair and small circle on the star; a faint radius line to each intended position; a short compass tick-arc either side of the ring where the radius meets it; one dimension line for the focused body, with a tick end, an extension past the ring and its planned value in dot-matrix numerals.
The defect
A second dimension, in ember, from the centre to the body's actual position, with the actual value placed beyond the body. Two numbers, one plan, one reality; nothing blinks.
Beats
ly-3: the centre mark, then the radius lines, then the arcs and the dimension draw in (a drafter's order); ly-4: the bodies land on the radius ends; df-2: the ember dimension appears as the body is seen off its line; up-2: ember radius lines across the neighbourhood.
Risks
The two dimension lines are 12° apart and both pass near the body; the values need the placement rule (planned value mid-line, actual value past the body) or they collide, as they did in the first cut. Radius lines through the system add clutter at mid detail. 850–973 dots close.

G · Tolerance bands

Description
Each orbit is a band of allowed range, ±0.45 units: two dotted edges and a sparse fill. A healthy body sits inside. The plan says "here, within this much".
The defect
The body is outside the band, and the outer edge goes ember over ±0.3 rad where it crossed. At the hood the ember arcs show the spread with no number.
Beats
ly-3: the bands draw in; ly-4: bodies inside them; df-2: the ember edge; df-5/up-2: ember arcs across the neighbourhood, more as you scroll.
Risks
Three bands are nine rings of dots: 1,344 close and, at the hood, they merge into a thick ring (one edge drawn there instead). The ember arc is the strongest hood signal of the round, but it sits on the ring, not on the body, so a reader can take it for a ring that has lit rather than a body that has left. Bands imply tolerance; the essay has not said tolerance, so an alt text would.

H · Mission plan

Description
Orbits as planned trajectories, in the manner of mission diagrams and manoeuvre nodes: from each intended position a dashed forward arc of 100°, node markers (small crosses) at 0°, 35° and 70° ahead, and a chevron at the arc's end for direction. The ring itself is faint.
The defect
The body is off its node, and from where it is an ember dashed arc curves outward with its own chevron: the path it is on instead.
Beats
ly-3: nodes, then arcs, then chevrons; ly-4: bodies on their first nodes; df-2: the deviating arc; up-2: ember arcs across the neighbourhood.
Risks
It is the most "spaceflight" and the least "blueprint": arcs and chevrons say manoeuvre, and nodes at set times say schedule, which the essay has no line for. Nodes at the hood become texture (3,731 dots). The ember arc is a strong signal but it is motion language, the closest of the round to monitoring.

B′ · Design hologram

The defect

Beats

C′ · Engineering

Description

R · Recommended combination

Description
B′'s plane and stalks (no grid), C′'s graduations and centre mark, E's placeholders, G's bands, and one F dimension on the focused body, drawn 1.2× bolder than the others. Bands say where; outlines say what size; stalks say the plane; the dimension says how far, in a number.
The defect
Four departures that are all the same fact: the body is outside its band (ember edge), beside its outline (empty), off its stalk (ember stalk), and a second ember dimension reads 9.4 past it where the plan says 8.5. At the hood, ember edges beside empty circles, no number.

If it is still too much, drop the stalks (the plane is already implied by the bands' ellipses) before dropping the outlines. The desktop hood needed its framing lowered to clear the band; the essay's own framing governs that.

8. R + H: the author's pick, with a hand-over

The author's decision after round 2: "I like R for the one-system view but prefer H for the neighbourhood view." So one concept, first in the swipe: R at full detail, H at the neighbourhood, and a cross-fade between them by projected size rather than a switch, so a zoom from the neighbourhood into one system morphs H's arcs into R's bands, outlines and stalks.

The hand-over band: 220 → 320 CSS px of the system's projected outer diameter (HANDOVER in mock.html; in the essay's s_px, which is the projected outer radius, that is 110 → 160). H's marks are drawn at 1 − w and R's at w, with w = smoothstep(220, 320, px). Below 220 px the system is wholly H; above 320 px wholly R. The band starts where H's chevrons are still readable (at 220 px an arc is about 90 px long) and ends under the essay's full-detail threshold (300 px here), so by the time the moons and tracks are whole the drawing is R's. In the mock the midpoint (hero at 275 px) is zoom 0.704; the band runs from zoom 0.568 to 0.818.

Scrubbing it. The zoom (slider) framing holds the neighbourhood model with the close framing's system at its centre and moves the camera continuously from the hood framing (0) to the close framing (1): distance log-linear, elevation 42° → 28°, azimuth 12° → −24°. A slider appears under the chips; the wheel scrubs too; ?f=zoom&z=0.7 or ?px=270 (ask for the hero's diameter in px) sets it from the URL; &solo=1 draws the hero alone to count its dots. The camera never moves in the other framings.

H's "planned positions at set times" in the essay's orbit model
The essay's bodies move on Kepler ellipses, mean anomaly M = phase·2π + 2π t/period solved by keplerE (orbit.ts/orbit.glsl), with the ambient clock as t. The plan is the orbit itself: a node at "k steps ahead" is orbitPos(t + k·period/12) evaluated in the same vertex path, so every node is true of the model and no second set of phases exists. A healthy body is on plan by construction. The forward arc is the same ellipse sampled from the body's current anomaly ahead by 100°, dashed by a hash on the sample index (planDash). Because t is the ambient, the nodes and the arc rotate with the body on the ambient clock and hold under ?test=1 and reduced motion like everything else; at the hourly wrap they jump with the orbits (accepted).
"Off plan" against the essay's defect states
The plan marks are drawn from the orbit without the derp; the body is drawn with it. So the gap between a body and its node, outline, band or stalk is exactly the derp the essay already computes (derp.ts: events on derpSeed/derpPhase for every body with cracked), and no new "actual versus planned" state is needed. By state: cracked (the data's lineage flag, from R0 by hop) draws nothing on its own, so "From far away, everything looks right" holds; lit (hiAt, the ember lamp by Lamp membership and net.highlight) turns the body's deviation arc, band edge and second dimension ember with the lamp, so df-4, df-5 and up-2 read as they do now, with the arcs in place of the round-1 triangles and no counter; derp (the continuous events, close only) is what moves the body off its marks, visible at df-1 if you watch and plain at df-2; freeze (FREEZE_PINS.C1, the pinned frame at df-2 with the off-path moon and its faint double) keeps its own marks, and the plan's empty moon outline beside the doubled moon is the same sentence as the alt's "marks drawn round both"; fix (fixAt, net.fix, the gold landing) returns the body into its outline and band and cools the ember marks to phosphor as the lamp goes out, fx-4 outward from the centre. The mock's DRIFT (0.22 rad on, 0.9 units out, 1.2× size) is a stand-in for the derp's reach; the port uses the derp and draws no drift of its own.
