The freeze frame: references

Twenty images collected for the moment we zoom in on the defect and the frame freezes, comic-book style. The stills belong to their owners. They are private references only: nothing here is shipped or traced. A design built from this board is being drawn now.

One-line direction

A held moment that moves "on twos" (each pose held for two frames). An inked outline of motes round the planet, its own two lights slightly out of register, and a dotted burst of ticks around the derping moon. The rest of the field dims and tips a few degrees, and a thin frame snaps shut round the picture. All of it is made of motes, none of it is lettering, and it can fade by the next scroll step.

Print and technique

What transfers to motes, best first

  1. Held frame, then "on twos": the motion stops, then steps at about 12 frames a second around the moon only.
  2. A dotted burst of ticks around the derping moon: straight or stepped ticks, not the film's wavy squiggles.
  3. Misregistration: the planet and moon drawn two or three times, a few pixels apart, in our own blue and ember, with the moon offset more.
  4. Coarse halftone on the planet body: big dots on the lit side and small at the edge of the shadow. It has to stay coarse on a phone.
  5. An inked outline of motes round the planet: thicker on the lit side, thin into the shade, broken where it starts.
  6. Hatching on the shadow side.
  7. The camera tips 3–6° and holds.
  8. The background dims and tints.
  9. A thin frame snaps in round the picture.
  10. A single-frame flash, rate-limited and off under reduced motion.
  11. Bright bubbles drifting off after the freeze.

What we will not use

ThingWhy not
Name card, tagline, impact words, caption boxesLabels in the picture; text stays at the top.
The wavy squiggle ring over a headThe film's signature mark: "in the vein of", never a copy.
A red-and-blue split paletteReads as the film's identity; we use our own blue and ember.
Grids of many panelsToo small to read on a phone.
Script lettering, skulls, any logo or characterDirect imitation of a known game or film.
Fine halftone and hatchingThey turn to noise on a phone, so they stay coarse.
