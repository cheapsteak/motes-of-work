# Look reference for the Act II centre: findings (condensed by the controller from the research agent's report)

Internal reference only. The images in img/ belong to NASA, ESO, CNRS, Warner Bros. and others. Never commit them.

## Author's chosen model (binding; it overrides the research's option A)

- **The black hole is all the rules taken together:** one continuous mass with no discrete parts. Every token is matter.
- **Prompts are the matter falling in.** They are never named in the text.
- **The one incorrect rule is a thread of bad matter, not a body.** It is a single ember strand spiralling in the disk among clean blue.
- **"Now the defect is in the rules too":** one agent's notes fall in as an ember stream and become that strand.
- **The broadcast:** ember knots fire up the jets. This is the bad rule going out to every agent.
- **The fix:** the strand cools to clean and the ember knots stop.

## Black hole cues, strongest first, and which survive on a phone

- **A Doppler-lopsided disk.** The approaching side is brighter and bluer-white; the receding side is dimmer and redder.
  - It vanishes face-on and reverses from below.
  - Interstellar removed the asymmetry for a calm look. A lopsided disk reads as spinning; a symmetric one reads as a logo.
- **The disk's far side lensed into an arch over the shadow,** plus a thin arc under it. This survives at phone size.
- **The photon ring:** one thin ring at about twice the horizon size. At about 120 pt across it is a 1–2 px line. A hand-drawn 1 px ring looked wrong in the Gargantua demo.
- **The shadow** must stay black. Mask glow and bloom out of it, because bloom leaks in.
- **Keplerian shear:** the inner disk turns about 35 times faster than the outer. Noise in the disk winds into rings narrower than a pixel. The Gargantua demo cross-fades two disks every 20 s.
- **Jets** start as a narrow parabola, then become a cone. Draw them thin at the base, blue or violet.
  - Knots are bright clumps that travel outward and form when matter falls in sporadically. Some are stationary shocks.
  - M87's jet wobbles about 10° on an 11-year cycle because of a tilted disk.
- **Infall** reads as streaks of 2–4 dots that tighten into the disk plane and speed up as they fall. A tidal-disruption stream is a long thread that wraps into the disk.
- **Luminet 1979** is a black hole drawn entirely in India-ink dots: density alone carries the brightness and the lopsidedness. This is the direct precedent for a mote-drawn core (img 04).
- **Phone limits.** Only 1 px points are guaranteed. Size points by device pixel ratio (capped at 2), set a minimum size and soften edges by ±0.5 px. The arch, the lopsidedness, the jets and the shear motion survive at phone size; nested rings and fine lanes don't.

## Making the classes read as different

- **Star (plan) versus planet (code): the terminator is the strongest cue.**
  - A star glows everywhere and darkens at its limb.
  - A planet is a crescent lit from its star's side and dark on the far side.
  - Glow only where it's hot (Universe Sandbox): the glow sprite on every class is part of why they all look alike.
- **Spikes mean a point light source;** longer spikes mean brighter (Webb 6+2, Hubble 4). They can be built from rows of dots.
- **Motion type is a strong class label** (Bartram & Ware): orbit versus outflow versus pulse separates groups better than direction does, and circular motion draws the eye.
- **Colour temperature:** stars run white to amber (blackbody, never green or purple); planets are cool, tinted by their star.
- **Mote grammar:**
  - **Core:** a sheared, lopsided dot disk with streaks, the arch, a dark shadow, and dot-column jets with knots.
  - **Plans (stars):** a dense core mote, a falloff halo and 4 or 6 dot-row spikes. They pulse but don't move across the frame.
  - **Code (planets):** the existing lattice sphere, kept for code only, with a crescent made by dimming the dots away from the star. A slow orbit and spin; moons faster.
